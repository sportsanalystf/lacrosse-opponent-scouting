JMU Defensive Scheme Analysis

JMU employs a zone-based defense with slide packages and help rotations. Analysis based on defensive clips (1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 12, 13, 14).

Defensive Formation Overview

Defensive Scheme Characteristics

  • Zone Type: 2-3 Zone (2 close attackmen, 3 help players)
  • Slide Package: Proactive slides from help-side when ball moves; midfield support on short stick
  • Recovery: Moderate recovery speed; vulnerabilities emerge when cutters attack before recovery complete
  • Help-Side Awareness: Gaps exist on backside; LSM not always helping on B-side throwbacks
  • Transition Setup: Slower to set in transition; not enough depth early

Slide System Breakdown

Crease Slide

When attacking near crease, JMU's close attackmen slide hard. Problem: other zones left open temporarily. Goal = quick kick-outs to open shooters.

Help-Side Slide

Help defenders (blue in diagram) slide to ball. Issue: backside left undefended. Exploit with backside/throwback passes.

Short Stick Support

Midfield short stick (yellow) provides central support. Struggles when motion is rapid; gap opens for interior cuts.

Transition Help

LSM (purple) helps transition but positioning can be reactive. Attack immediately in transition before LSM is set.

Zone Gap Exploitation Strategy

Detailed analysis of clips 7-8: driving through JMU's zone defensive gaps. These clips show clear lanes for hard ball-carriers to attack.

Zone Gap Locations

Gap Exploitation Tactics (Clips 7-8 Breakdown)

Gap 1: Top/Upper Zone

Attack: Drive hard up the side, between top close attackman and midfield help.

Why it works: JMU's help defenders take away the crease; side gaps open. Player #18 exploited this in clip 7.

Finish: Score or kick to opposite pole.

Gap 2: Bottom/Lower Zone

Attack: Mirror of Gap 1. Drive low, between bottom close attackman and help.

Why it works: Same principle: help focuses on crease/ball, sides open.

Finish: Score or dish to cutting teammate.

Gap 3: Crease Vertical

Attack: Drive straight down the crease, knife attack.

Why it works: If attacking from wings, crease becomes open window. JMU's close attackmen cannot cover both sides simultaneously.

Finish: High-quality inside scoring opportunity.

Combo: Gap Shift

Attack: Fake one gap, attack another. Motion to misdirect, then hard drive through primary gap.

Why it works: JMU's zone will collapse toward misdirection; real attack finds opening.

Finish: Higher-quality shot or skip shot opportunity.

Player #18 Tendency (Clip 7): In clip 7, player #18 successfully drives through zone gaps with pace. This indicates #18 is a scoring threat on hard drives. UVA should mirror similar drive-heavy attacks. Keep #18 in view for game film review to understand his footwork and angle selection.

Success Metrics for Gap Exploitation

Shot Quality High-percentage looks; GLE, crease, or near-post opportunity
Drive Speed Attack with intent; slow drives allow help to recover. Hard pace beats slides.
Timing First 5 seconds of possession before JMU fully set (see Breakdown Analysis tab)
Catch-and-Drive Don't pause with ball at top. Catch and drive immediately through gap.

Transition & Man-Up Defense Analysis

JMU's vulnerability in unset situations and player-down defending. Clips 10-11 (transition goals), 12-14 (man-up schemes).

Transition Defense (Clips 10-11: Both GOALS)

Critical Insight: JMU allowed 2 goals in transition situations. This is a major weakness. Recommendation: Attack in transition every possession opportunity. Do not wait for full offense to set.

Transition Attack Principles

  • 1 Ground Balls = Transition Opportunity Every ground ball is a chance to attack in transition. Push numbers early.
  • 2 Create Numbers Advantage If you have 3v2 or 2v1, attack immediately. Don't reset to settled offense.
  • 3 Fast Ball Movement No extra touches. One or two passes max before shooting.
  • 4 Avoid Defensive Support If JMU's help has arrived, reset and set offense. But in first 3 seconds, attack.

Man-Up Defense Analysis (Clips 12, 13, 14)

JMU defends man-up situations (4v5, extra man offense) with multiple formations. Clip 14 shows "2-in-the-middle" variant.

Attack Strategies vs JMU Man-Up Defense

Attack the Wings

2-in-the-middle formation leaves wings relatively open. Use your extra man (5v4) to overload wings. Quick swing passes to open wing shooters.

High Pick & Roll

Set picks at top of zone to free up ball-carrier. JMU's compact middle will have to adjust; gaps open.

Crease Attacks

If wing is covered, kick-down to crease attacker. JMU's help cannot cover all areas with 4v5.

Drive & Dump

Drive hard at middle defenders; they collapse. Dump to open teammate on perimeter or in crease.

Man-Up Scoring Sequence

Phase 1 (First 3 seconds): Aggressive ball movement. Swing to wings or attack crease. JMU's 4 defenders cannot cover all 5 attackers.

Phase 2 (4-8 seconds): If initial attack doesn't yield shot, reset and re-attack different area (e.g., if first attack was wing, second is crease).

Phase 3 (9+ seconds): Take best available shot. JMU will have settled into their formation by now.

Key: Score within first 10 seconds of man-up possession. Goal rate improves with early aggression.

UVA Attack Game Plan vs JMU Defense

Priority-ranked offensive recommendations based on 14-clip scouting analysis. Deploy in order of effectiveness.

Priority 1: MUST EXECUTE (Highest % Success)

  • 1A Motion → Drive/Cut Transition

    Run motion offense but be READY to drive or cut aggressively. JMU's slides are late on action. As soon as defender commits, ball-carrier attacks or interior cutter attacks. 3 goals allowed in film; this is their #1 weakness.

  • 1B Immediate Transition Attack

    Every ground ball is a scoring opportunity. Attack with numbers advantage before JMU sets. Push 2-on-1, 3-on-2 aggressively. 2 goals in transition in film; they cannot defend unset.

Priority 2: HIGH PROBABILITY (Secondary Weapons)

  • 2A B-Side Throwback Attack

    Use backside throwback passes to catch JMU's defense rotated. Goal allowed on this in film. When help-side is engaged, swing ball to opposite side quickly. Open shooters available on weakside.

  • 2B Drive Through Zone Gaps

    Attack upper and lower zone gaps with hard drives. Gaps 1, 2, 3 (see Zone Gap tab) are exploitable. Not goal-heavy in sample but high-quality shot opportunities. Build into complementary set.

Priority 3: SITUATIONAL (Set Plays)

  • 3A Man-Up Wing Attack

    In man-up situations, attack wings to exploit 2-in-the-middle formation (clip 14 formation). Quick swing to open wing. 5v4 gives clear advantage on perimeter.

  • 3B Pure Motion (Secondary)

    Pure motion without action is least effective; JMU handles it reasonably (3 clips, no goals). Use as misdirection BEFORE executing Priority 1 or 2 attacks.

Quick Reference: Situation Matrix

→ Run Priority 1A: Motion → Drive/Cut
→ Be ready to attack on 2nd or 3rd touch
→ Don't let ball just move; create action

→ Priority 1B: Transition Attack
→ Push numbers immediately
→ 2-on-1 or 3-on-2, finish quick
→ Don't reset to set offense

Scenario: Help Rotating Hard

→ Priority 2A: B-Side Throwback
→ Swing opposite immediately
→ Open shooter on weakside

Scenario: Man-Up (Extra Man)

→ Priority 3A: Wing Attack
→ Attack wings before crease
→ 2-in-middle leaves wings open
→ Quick swing, open shooter

Scenario: Early Shot Clock (12+ sec)

→ Priority 2B: Drive Zone Gaps
→ Create shooting opportunity via drive
→ Set secondary attack if no shot
→ Goal is opening for teammate

Scenario: Nothing Working

→ Return to Priority 1A
→ Motion to reset, then drive/cut
→ If still stalled: clear and restart

In-Game Checkpoints

Q1/Q2 Focus: Test Priority 1A and 1B. Establish that JMU cannot defend motion-to-action transitions or transition attacks. Build confidence and score early.

Q2/Q3 Transition: If 1A/1B working, increase frequency. If JMU adjusts, pivot to 2A/2B. Mix sets to prevent them from settling into defensive rhythm.

Q4 Crunch: Default to Priority 1B (transition). Fastest scoring method. Move the ball to 2v1 or 3v2 situations. If game close, this is safest bet.

Two-Minute Drill: Eliminate mistakes. High-percentage sets only. Settled motion-to-drive (1A) or transition push (1B). No fancy sets.
